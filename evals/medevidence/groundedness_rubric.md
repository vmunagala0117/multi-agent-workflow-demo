# MedEvidence Groundedness Rubric

## supported

Every material part of the claim is explicitly supported by the supplied
evidence. Paraphrasing is allowed. The claim must not strengthen causality,
certainty, population scope, duration, or safety beyond the evidence.

## partial

The evidence supports the core claim, but at least one material qualifier is
missing, overstated, or broader than the evidence. Examples include changing
"observed in the study population" to "works for all adults" or presenting an
unresolved association as proven causation.

## unsupported

The supplied evidence does not establish the claim, or it contradicts a
material part of the claim. Keyword overlap alone is not support.

## Labeling Rules

1. Judge only the claim and evidence supplied in the example.
2. Do not use outside medical knowledge.
3. Evaluate all material qualifiers, including population, intervention,
   outcome, timeframe, magnitude, certainty, and safety.
4. If one material clause is supported and another is not, label the complete
   claim `partial` unless the unsupported clause reverses the central meaning.
5. Record a concise rationale that points to the decisive support or gap.
